# unlose Trademark Policy

**Version 1.0 (2026-08-09)**

The **unlose** name and logo are trademarks of the unlose project ("we", "our").

We are an open-source project (Apache-2.0) and we want you to use our name and logo
freely — as long as you use them in ways that are honest, clear, and never confusing
or deceptive. This policy explains exactly what you can and cannot do.

This policy is written in the spirit of the [Mozilla Trademark Policy](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/foundation/trademarks/policy/)
and covers the same situations with the same principles: **be clear about who you
are and what you are talking about.**

---

## 1. Covered marks

This policy covers the word mark **unlose**
and the unlose logo (the shield/time-machine glyph shipped in the repository under
`src/Unlose.UI/Resources/`).

It does **not** cover third-party names, marks, or logos that may appear in our
code, documentation, or tests (e.g., names of other projects such as Claude Code,
DCG, or VSS).

## 2. What you can do without asking (CAN)

You may use the unlose name and logo, without our written permission, when:

- **Talking about unlose honestly.** In blog posts, articles, videos, talks, social
  media, or anywhere you discuss the project — including critical reviews. A
  truthful statement such as "works with unlose" or "an alternative to unlose" is
  always fine.
- **Forking and modifying the code for yourself.** You may keep the name and logo
  in your own fork, private builds, or patches — as long as you never publish or
  distribute them as if they were official.
- **Building on top of unlose.** You may say your product or service "supports
  unlose", "is compatible with unlose", or "integrates with unlose", when that is
  truthful. Keep the unlose name visually distinct from your own branding.
- **Quoting or reproducing docs.** You may quote, link to, and mirror our
  documentation, keeping the original name and logo, provided you do not imply
  official publication.
- **Contributing.** You may use the name and logo in pull requests, issues, and
  community discussion about the project itself.

## 3. What you cannot do without asking (CANNOT)

You may **not** use the unlose name or logo, without our written permission, when:

- **Confusing people about the source.** Do not use "unlose" as the name of your
  own project, product, service, fork, or distribution, or in a way that makes
  people think your work is official, endorsed, or maintained by us.
- **Impersonating official builds.** Do not distribute modified or repackaged
  binaries, installers, or packages under the "unlose" name. Official builds are
  the signed MSI from the official repository or website only.
- **Using the logo as your own.** Do not use the unlose logo (or a confusingly
  similar mark) as the logo, icon, favicon, or branding of your own project.
- **Domain and package squatting.** Do not register domains, GitHub orgs, npm,
  PyPI, or other package names that combine "unlose" with your own branding in a
  way that implies official status (e.g. `unlose-official`, `unlose-pro`).
- **Misrepresenting security guarantees.** Do not use the name in a way that
  implies unlose does something it does not (e.g. blocking commands — unlose is a
  snapshot safety net, not an interceptor).
- **Selling the name.** Do not sell, license, or transfer the name or logo, or
  domain names containing "unlose", to third parties.

## 4. Forking: rename it

If you create a derivative work (fork, port, rewrite, or repackage), you **must
choose a different name** for your project. "Fork it, but rename it" is a hard
rule, not a suggestion:

- Pick a name that is clearly distinct from "unlose" — not a variation such as
  "unlosed", "unloser", "unlose-pro", "unlose fork", or similar.
- Your project may honestly state that it is "a fork of unlose" in its README or
  about page, but it must not present itself as unlose itself.
- If you ship a derivative, the community (and we) will thank you for your
  honesty — confusion hurts everyone.

## 5. Other legal terms

- "unlose" is used here as a trademark claim, not a warranty. The Apache-2.0
  license covers code only; the name and logo are **not** granted by the license.
- If you have a legitimate need that is not covered above (e.g., a book title, a
  course, a commercial integration that wants prominent co-branding), ask us:
  **trademark@unlose.dev** — we are friendly and usually say yes.
- We reserve the right to update this policy. When we do, we will update this file
  and note the change in the release notes.

## 6. Report abuse

If you see someone distributing malicious or modified software under the "unlose"
name, please report it: **security@unlose.dev** (or GitHub private advisory).
Official builds are only the signed MSI from the official repository and
**unlose.app**.
